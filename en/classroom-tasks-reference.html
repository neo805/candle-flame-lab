Task 1 · Grid chemistry: stoichiometry and enthalpy for six reactions

Goal: complete the species changes and reaction enthalpies for candle combustion. Each reaction states which species are consumed or produced, in what amounts, and whether the reaction releases or absorbs heat.
Read the model and instructions

Candle combustion is split into six reactions. R1 is a complete example; follow it to complete R2–R6. Every assignment has the form after[species.NAME] += coefficient * reactionExtent;.

  1. Use a negative coefficient for a consumed species and a positive coefficient for a produced species.
  2. For an exothermic reaction, add a negative enthalpy contribution; for an endothermic process such as evaporation or pyrolysis, use a positive value.
  3. Enthalpy is in J/mol: write values such as -1255500.0, not kJ. R3 splits one C₂₅H₅₂ into 12.5 C₂H₂ and 13.5 H₂; R6 splits one C₂H₂ into 2 C and 1 H₂.

Each reaction is independent. Variables such as the acetylene oxidation extent are already computed; do not change them. Keep the entire region valid JavaScript.

Task 2 · Neighbor flow: normalized weights in four directions

Goal: determine how much gas exchanges in each direction. Flow weight = area weight + buoyancy contribution + gas-advection contribution; normalize by the sum of all four weights so the fractions total exactly 1.
Read the geometry and instructions

The two axial faces have the same area as the cell cross-section, so their area weights are both 1.0. The inner and outer radial faces are weighted by distance from the axis; the outer face is larger.

  1. Choose four of the eight candidate expressions below for the first four blanks: top, bottom, inner, and outer area weights. Click a candidate to copy it.
  2. Add the cell's upward buoyancy contribution in the upward direction and subtract its downward contribution in the downward direction. Advection contributes only in the direction toward which the gas blows; these contributions are already calculated.
  3. Complete the sum of the four flow weights, then divide each directional weight by that sum in the four corresponding branches. Do not change the prepared flow terms or output code.

The diagram shows the third radial cell column from the axis. Its sector angle is enlarged to 12° for visibility; the actual angle is about 5.7°.

Task 3 · Main simulation loop: advance step by step

Goal: complete the outer loop. Each iteration is one simulation step and performs three operations in order—gas transport, temperature diffusion, and chemical reaction—with every grid cell processed in each operation.
Read the algorithm and instructions

Stage ① is complete. Follow it to complete stages ② and ③, and supply the outer loop's condition and increment. Available functions are diffuseTemperature(sim, gx, gy) and gridChemistry(sim, gx, gy).

  1. Use rows gy = 1 through ny - 2 and columns gx = 1 through nx - 1, exactly as in stage ①.
  2. The outer loop has the form for (var i = 0; condition; increment). Continue while more of the requested n steps remain and increment i once per iteration.
  3. Call the temperature-diffusion function in stage ② and the grid-chemistry function in stage ③. Preserve buffer swaps and the begin/finish calls.

The all-placeholder version performs one safe step. Your completed version must execute every requested step, which is essential for meaningful 300–600 step tests.
